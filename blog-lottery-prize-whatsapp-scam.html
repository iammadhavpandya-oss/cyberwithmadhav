Lottery and Prize Scams on WhatsApp

You never entered a lucky draw, yet a message says you won a car, cash or a KBC prize. You just need to pay a small fee first. That fee is the scam.

Quick answer

If you did not enter a contest, you did not win it. Any "prize" that asks you to pay tax, processing, GST or delivery fees first is a scam. Do not pay, do not share documents or OTPs, and report the number on Sanchar Saathi Chakshu. If you already paid, call 1930.

How common it is

In the GSMA India Consumer Scam Report 2026, prize, lottery and inheritance scams were the second most common type among Indian victims surveyed, at 31%.

How it works

  1. A WhatsApp message, call or SMS says you won a lucky draw, often using a famous TV show, brand or company name.
  2. It may include a photo of a cheque, a "certificate" or a person in a suit.
  3. To "release" the prize, you must pay a fee: tax, GST, processing, courier or bank charges.
  4. After the first payment, new fees keep appearing until you stop.

Red flags

  • You never entered any contest.
  • Payment is needed before you receive anything.
  • They ask for Aadhaar, PAN or bank details on chat.
  • The number has a foreign code, or a normal mobile number pretends to be a company.
  • "Keep this secret" or "offer ends today".

Did you win a lottery? Pause first: did you even buy a ticket?

What to do with such a message

  • Do not reply, click or call back.
  • Report it on Sanchar Saathi Chakshu. Chakshu accepts calls, SMS and WhatsApp messages.
  • Block and report the number inside WhatsApp.
  • Warn elderly family members, who are often targeted.

What to do if it already happened

  1. Stop paying. More fees will never unlock a prize.
  2. Call 1930 and complete the complaint at cybercrime.gov.in.
  3. If you shared ID documents, watch for misuse, and check the mobile connections in your name on Sanchar Saathi.
  4. Save chats, numbers and payment receipts.

Key takeaways

  • No entry means no prize.
  • A real prize never asks you to pay first.
  • Famous show or company names and logos do not make a message real.
  • Report on Chakshu; call 1930 if you paid.
