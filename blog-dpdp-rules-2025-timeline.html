DPDP Rules 2025 Timeline: What Applies Now and What Starts on 13 May 2027

India's data protection law is coming into force in phases. One phase began this week. Here is a clear, sourced timeline of what applies now, what comes next, and what it means for you and for businesses.

Quick answer

The DPDP Rules, 2025 were notified on 13 November 2025 with an 18 month phased rollout. Phase 1 (from 13 November 2025) set up the Data Protection Board. Phase 2 (from 13 November 2026) started the Consent Manager registration framework, with the portal dpbi.gov.in. Phase 3 (from 13 May 2027) brings most duties for organisations and rights for individuals into force, including notice, consent, security safeguards and breach reporting.

DPDP in one paragraph

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 governs how organisations process digital personal data in India. Individuals are called Data Principals. Organisations deciding why and how data is processed are Data Fiduciaries. The Act gives individuals rights, such as access, correction and erasure, and puts duties on organisations, such as lawful processing, purpose limitation, security safeguards and breach notification. The Data Protection Board of India handles violations and can impose penalties. MeitY told the Lok Sabha in August 2026 that penalties can go up to ₹250 crore, depending on factors like the nature of the breach and harm caused.

The three phase timeline

The commencement notification G.S.R. 843(E), dated 13 November 2025, and MeitY's statements describe three phases:

  • Phase 1, from 13 November 2025: definitions, provisions about the Data Protection Board and related sections came into force on publication. This is about setting up and operating the Board.
  • Phase 2, one year later, from 13 November 2026: provisions about Consent Managers came into force. On 14 November 2026, PIB reported that MeitY launched the portal dpbi.gov.in for eligible companies to apply for registration.
  • Phase 3, eighteen months after notification, from 13 May 2027: the core duties of Data Fiduciaries, the rights of Data Principals, and the Board's inquiry and penalty processes come into force.

What is a Consent Manager?

A Consent Manager is an entity registered with the Data Protection Board that lets people give, manage, review and withdraw consent through one accessible, transparent and interoperable platform. According to PIB's 14 November 2026 release, only companies incorporated in India with adequate technical, operational and financial capacity, including a net worth of at least ₹2 crore, can apply. The Board will publish the list of registered Consent Managers on its website.

For everyday users, this could eventually mean one dashboard to see and withdraw consents given to different apps, instead of hunting through each app's settings.

What starts on 13 May 2027

From this date, most of the protections people care about apply. Key duties for organisations include:

  • Clear notice: PIB's release on the Rules says Data Fiduciaries must give standalone, clear and simple consent notices explaining the specific purpose of data collection.
  • Valid consent for processing where consent is the basis, and easy withdrawal.
  • Reasonable security safeguards, such as encryption or masking, as MeitY described to Parliament.
  • Breach intimation: Rule 7 of the DPDP Rules requires informing affected individuals without delay, and informing the Board without delay, with a detailed report within 72 hours unless the Board allows more time.
  • Erasure of personal data when consent is withdrawn or the purpose is served, unless the law requires retention.

What it means for you as an individual

Until 13 May 2027, most of these rights are not yet enforceable under the DPDP framework, but you can already practise good habits: share less data, read consent screens, and remove apps you do not use. After May 2027, you should expect clearer notices, the ability to withdraw consent more easily, and breach notifications if your data is affected.

What businesses should do now

Six months is not a lot of time. A practical checklist:

  1. Map your data: what personal data you collect, why, where it is stored and who can access it.
  2. Fix notices and consent flows to be clear, specific and easy to withdraw.
  3. Strengthen security: access control, encryption, logging and secret management. CERT-In's 2026 advisories show how often basic weaknesses lead to data exposure.
  4. Prepare a breach response plan with a 72 hour reporting track for the Board.
  5. Review vendors who process data for you.
  6. Train staff. Most data incidents involve people and processes, not just technology.

For law and security students

DPDP sits exactly where law meets security, and it creates real jobs in privacy, GRC and security engineering. If you are studying law like me, or cybersecurity, learn both sides: read the Act and Rules on official sites, and understand how security controls make compliance possible. Key takeaways

  • DPDP rollout has three phases over 18 months from 13 November 2025.
  • Consent Manager registration opened on 13 November 2026.
  • Most obligations and individual rights start on 13 May 2027.
  • Businesses should prepare now: data mapping, notices, security and a breach plan.
