HSRP Number Plate Booking Scams

Many vehicle owners search online to book a High Security Registration Plate. Scammers know this, and run fake booking sites, ads and messages that look official.

Quick answer

Book HSRP only through the link given on your state transport department's official website or your vehicle dealer, never through an ad, an SMS link or a WhatsApp forward. No genuine booking needs a separate "verification app" that reads your SMS. If you paid a fake site, call your bank and 1930 right away.

What HSRP is, in one paragraph

HSRP stands for High Security Registration Plate. It is a tamper proof number plate with security features such as a hologram and a laser branded number, fixed with snap locks. Many states have asked owners of older vehicles to fit one, which is why so many people search for "HSRP booking" online.

How the HSRP scam works

According to the Seqrite India Cyber Threat Report 2026, HSRP scams copy official booking portals and promote them through search ads, SMS and WhatsApp forwards. Common patterns:

  • A look alike website takes your vehicle details and payment, but no plate is ever fitted.
  • The site charges more than the real fee, or adds fake "urgent fitment" charges.
  • A message pushes you to install an "HSRP verification app" that asks for SMS or call permissions, so it can read OTPs and misuse UPI.
  • Messages threaten heavy fines "from tomorrow" to rush you.

How to book safely

  1. Open your state transport department's official website by typing the address yourself, or go through parivahan.gov.in.
  2. Follow the HSRP link that the official site gives. For example, the Rajasthan transport department page explains its own process.
  3. If you are unsure, ask your vehicle dealer or the local RTO which booking site is official in your state.
  4. Before paying, read the address bar again. Pay only on the page you reached from the official site.
  5. Keep the receipt and booking slip.

Red flags

  • The site came from a sponsored search result or a forwarded message.
  • It asks you to install an app or share an OTP for "verification".
  • It asks for payment by personal UPI ID or a QR code sent on chat.
  • Prices or deadlines look very different from what your state's official page says.

What to do if it already happened

  1. Call your bank on the number printed on your card and block the card or UPI if details were shared.
  2. Call 1930 and then complete the complaint on cybercrime.gov.in with the transaction ID.
  3. If you installed an app, follow the steps in our fake mParivahan APK guide: airplane mode, uninstall, change passwords.
  4. Save screenshots of the website, the address bar and the payment screen.

Key takeaways

  • Start from your state transport department's official website, not from a search ad.
  • Fake sites copy logos and colours, so check the web address carefully.
  • Never install an "HSRP app" from a link, and never share an OTP for booking.
  • If you paid a fake site, block your card or UPI and report on 1930.
