A 90 Day Cybersecurity Study Plan for Beginners (Free Resources)

You have seen the whole map in this series. Now let us turn it into a schedule. This plan assumes about one to two hours a day, and it works whether you are a student or working somewhere else.

Quick answer

Spend days 1 to 30 on foundations (networking, Linux, how the internet works), days 31 to 60 on security concepts and your home lab (attacks, defences, logs, Wireshark, nmap on your own lab), and days 61 to 90 on a chosen direction plus proof (CTFs, writeups, a portfolio and one beginner certificate). Study one to two hours a day and keep notes.

Ground rules before you start

  • Consistency over intensity. One focused hour daily beats eight hours on Sunday.
  • Notes in your own words. A simple notebook or markdown files on GitHub.
  • Practise legally. Only your own lab, CTF platforms and sites built for practice.
  • One weekly review. Every Sunday, revise the week and plan the next.
  • Rest. Burnout helps nobody. Take one easy day per week.

Days 1 to 30: Foundations

Week 1: What security is, and how computers talk. Read Parts 1 to 3 of this series. Learn IP addresses, ports and DNS. Use the subnet calculator daily for 10 minutes.

Week 2: How the internet works. Read Parts 5 and 6. Use browser developer tools to watch requests. Explain the OSI model to a friend or family member in simple words.

Week 3: Linux basics. Read Part 4. Practise navigation, files, grep and permissions every day. Start OverTheWire Bandit.

Week 4: Linux plus networking. Finish at least 10 Bandit levels. Practise ip a, ss, ping and nslookup. Play the terminal wargame on this site.

Days 31 to 60: Security concepts and your lab

Week 5: Build the lab. Follow Part 7. Set up one workstation VM and one vulnerable target on an isolated network. Take snapshots.

Week 6: Attacks and defences. Learn phishing, malware types, password attacks and web basics. Read our password hashing guide. Analyse one phishing email from your own inbox safely.

Week 7: Tools on your own lab. Use Wireshark on lab traffic and nmap against your own target. Read the target's logs after each scan.

Week 8: Logs and detection. Study Linux auth logs and Windows event logs. Write a tiny script that counts failed logins. Read about the MITRE ATT&CK framework at a high level.

Days 61 to 90: Direction and proof

Week 9: Choose a direction. Re read Part 2. Pick SOC, cloud, AppSec or GRC for the next few months.

Week 10: CTFs. Solve at least five beginner challenges on picoCTF or the CTF Arena. Write three writeups.

Week 11: Portfolio. Follow Part 10. Publish your lab writeup, CTF writeups and one small project on GitHub with good READMEs. Update LinkedIn as in Part 11.

Week 12: Certificate preparation. Start one free or low cost beginner certificate from Part 8. Review all your notes. Read Part 14 so you know the legal basics.

Week 13 (days 85 to 90): Review and plan. Take stock of what you learned and write a public "90 days of cybersecurity" post. Plan your next 90 days.

What a typical day looks like

  • 10 minutes: revise yesterday's notes.
  • 40 minutes: learn one new topic (video, course module or article).
  • 30 minutes: hands on practice (lab, wargame or CTF).
  • 10 minutes: write notes and one line in your progress log.

On busy days, do just the 10 minute revision and 20 minutes of practice. Chain mat todna, streak chalu rakho.

After 90 days

You will not be an expert, and that is fine. You will have a working lab, real notes, solved challenges, a small portfolio and a clear direction. That is exactly what interviewers for entry level roles want to see. Keep going with deeper topics in your chosen path, and come back to the CyberWithMadhav blog for new guides. All the best, aap kar sakte ho.

Frequently asked questions

I am working full time. Can I still follow this? Yes. Stretch it to 120 or 150 days if needed. The order matters more than the speed.

Do I need to buy any course? No. Every step in this plan can be done with free resources: Cisco Networking Academy, OverTheWire, picoCTF, official documentation and this site. Paid courses are optional.

What if I miss a week? Do not restart from zero. Do a quick revision day, then continue from where you stopped. Progress is not a straight line, and that is perfectly normal.

Key takeaways

  • Days 1 to 30: networking, Linux and internet basics.
  • Days 31 to 60: security concepts, home lab, logs and tools on your own lab.
  • Days 61 to 90: choose a direction, do CTFs, build a portfolio and prepare for a certificate.
  • Consistency beats intensity: one to two focused hours a day is enough.
