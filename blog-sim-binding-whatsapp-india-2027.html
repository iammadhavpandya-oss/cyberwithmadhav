SIM Binding for WhatsApp and Telegram in India: What Changes From 2027

India is making messaging apps stay tied to the actual SIM card in your phone, and log out web sessions regularly. The goal is to stop scammers running Indian numbers from abroad. Here is what was announced and what may change for you.

Quick answer

On 28 November 2025, the Department of Telecommunications issued directions under the Telecom Cyber Security Rules, 2024 (as amended) to app based communication services that use Indian mobile numbers, including WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal and others. Apps must stay continuously linked to the active SIM in the device, and web sessions must log out at least every 6 hours. News reports in October 2026 said enforcement is set for 1 January 2027.

What the directions say

According to PIB's release of 1 December 2025, DoT observed that some app based communication services let users keep using the service even when the SIM linked to the number is not in the device. It said this was being misused for cyber fraud, especially from outside India.

DoT issued directions on 28 November 2025 under the Telecom Cyber Security (TCS) Rules, 2024 (as amended) to services including WhatsApp, Telegram, Snapchat, Arattai, Sharechat, Josh, Jiochat and Signal. The directions require them to:

  1. Ensure the app is continuously linked to the SIM card associated with the registered mobile number, making it impossible to use the app without that specific, active SIM in the device.
  2. Ensure web instances log out periodically, not later than every 6 hours, with an option to re link using a QR code.

The original directions gave 90 days for implementation and 120 days for a compliance report.

Why the government says it is needed

PIB's release explains that accounts on messaging apps continue to work even after the SIM is removed, deactivated or moved abroad. That allows anonymous scams, remote digital arrest frauds and government impersonation calls using Indian numbers. Long lived web sessions also let fraudsters control accounts from far away after authenticating once.

The release notes that device binding and automatic logout are already standard in Indian banking and UPI apps, and cites cyber fraud losses exceeding ₹22,800 crore in 2024. It also clarifies that the direction does not affect users who are roaming with their SIM present in the handset.

The timeline in 2026 and 2027

The New Indian Express reported on 22 October 2026 that DoT is set to make SIM binding mandatory from 1 January 2027, with a compliance deadline of 31 December 2026, quoting a DoT official. The same report said web and desktop versions will face mandatory logouts every six hours, and mentioned reports that Snapchat is exempt. Treat details from news reports as subject to change, and check official DoT and PIB updates as the date approaches.

What may change for you

  • Keep your SIM in the phone where you use the app. If you use WhatsApp on a phone without the SIM for that number, for example a second phone on WiFi, it may stop working.
  • Keep the number active. If your SIM is deactivated for not recharging, the app linked to it may be blocked.
  • WhatsApp Web and desktop users may need to scan the QR code again more often.
  • Travellers: the release says roaming with the SIM in your handset is not affected.
  • Changing phones: move the SIM with the app, and follow the app's official instructions.

Will this stop scams?

It should make some scams harder, especially those that use Indian numbers from abroad or hijacked web sessions. But scammers adapt. They may move to other channels, use mule SIMs or social engineering. So your personal defences still matter:

  • WhatsApp two step verification PIN (see our takeover guide).
  • Check linked devices regularly.
  • Verify money requests by calling the person.
  • Report suspicious calls and messages on Sanchar Saathi's Chakshu.

The debate

Some users and experts have raised concerns about convenience, multi device use and privacy, while telecom operators have supported the move, according to news coverage. As a learner, it is a good case study in the trade off between security, usability and privacy, a balance that appears in almost every security decision.

Quick checklist before January 2027

  1. Is the SIM for your WhatsApp number active and inside the phone you use?
  2. Do you have two step verification turned on?
  3. Do you know which devices are linked to your account?
  4. Have you told elders that "app band ho gaya" messages asking them to click links or share codes are scams?

Rules help, habits protect. Dono zaroori hain.

Key takeaways

  • Messaging apps must stay linked to the active SIM of the registered number.
  • Web and desktop sessions must log out at least every 6 hours and re link by QR code.
  • The aim is to stop scams run from abroad using Indian numbers.
  • Reports say enforcement starts 1 January 2027. Keep your SIM active and in your phone.
