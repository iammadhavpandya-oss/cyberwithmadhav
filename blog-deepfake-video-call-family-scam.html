Deepfake Video Calls Asking Family for Money

Scammers can now fake a face and voice on a short video call. The story is always urgent: an accident, an arrest, a hospital bill. A simple family plan beats the technology.

Quick answer

If a video or voice call from a "relative" asks for money urgently, hang up and call them back on the number you already have saved. Ask a question only the real person would know, or use a family code word. Never pay because of a short, shaky video call. If you already paid, call 1930 now.

How the scam works

  1. You get a WhatsApp video or voice call. The face or voice looks like your son, daughter, sibling or friend.
  2. The call is short, often blurry, with "bad network" excuses.
  3. They say there is an emergency: accident, police case, hospital, stuck abroad.
  4. They ask you to send money now, to a UPI ID or account you do not know, and not to tell anyone.

Sometimes the call comes from an unknown number, and sometimes from the relative's real account after it was taken over. Our guide on WhatsApp account takeover explains how that happens.

Why it feels so real

Short clips of people's faces and voices are easy to find on social media. AI tools can turn them into a fake face or voice. Read more in our AI voice cloning guide. Scammers add fear and hurry, so you do not stop to check.

Red flags

  • Money is needed right now, and you must keep it secret.
  • The video is very short, frozen, blurry or the lips do not match.
  • They refuse to switch to a normal call or let you call back.
  • Payment goes to a stranger's UPI ID or account.
  • Someone else "takes over" the call, claiming to be police, doctor or lawyer.

A simple family plan

  1. Code word: pick a word only your family knows. Ask for it on any emergency call.
  2. Call back rule: always hang up and call the person back on the number already saved in your phone, or call another family member.
  3. No secrets rule: agree that nobody in the family will ever say "don't tell anyone" about money.
  4. Lock your WhatsApp: turn on two step verification, so it is harder to steal accounts.

First cut the call, then call them back yourself. In a real emergency, a 2 minute check does no harm.

What to do if it already happened

  1. Call 1930 immediately and then your bank.
  2. Complete the complaint at cybercrime.gov.in with the UPI ID, account number and call details.
  3. Save screenshots of the call log, chats and payment.
  4. Report the calling number on Sanchar Saathi Chakshu, and inside WhatsApp.
  5. Tell the real relative so they can secure their account and warn others.

Key takeaways

  • Urgency plus secrecy plus money equals stop.
  • Hang up and call back on the saved number.
  • Agree a family code word today.
  • Short, blurry, "bad network" video calls are a warning sign.

Official sources

  • cybercrime.gov.in: National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal
  • Sanchar Saathi Chakshu: report suspected fraud communication
  • 1930: National Cyber Crime Helpline
