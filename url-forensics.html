$ echo "$URL" | ./dissect --no-follow

Phishing URL Forensics

Paste a link, or the whole SMS or WhatsApp message that contains it. See who really owns the domain, whether it imitates an Indian bank, UPI app or government portal, and what is hidden inside redirects and encoding.

Fully offline. The link is never opened. Nothing is fetched, previewed or expanded, and nothing leaves your browser. That is why short links like bit.ly stay hidden here: expanding them would mean contacting the shortener.

Read a link like an analyst

  • Find the registrable domain: read the host from right to left. In hdfcbank.com.secure-login.example the owner is secure-login.example, not HDFC Bank.
  • Banks now use .bank.in, which only RBI-regulated banks can register through IDRBT (RBI circular of 22 April 2025). Government portals end in .gov.in or .nic.in. Read more in the .bank.in guide.
  • Lookalike characters: Cyrillic "а" and Latin "a" look the same. Browsers show such domains as punycode (xn--) in many cases, which this tool decodes.
  • APK links install apps outside the Play Store. Fake e-challan, KYC and wedding invite APKs are used to steal OTPs.

Limits

  • The tool judges the text of the link only. It cannot see the page, and a clean result does not mean a site is safe.
  • Lookalike detection compares against a list of official domains of common Indian banks, payment apps, government portals and big brands. Unlisted brands are not covered.
