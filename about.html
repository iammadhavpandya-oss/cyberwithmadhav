Who is Madhav Pandya?

Hi, I'm Madhav Pandya. I live in Vasai, Mumbai, and I started CyberWithMadhav so that beginners can learn cybersecurity and families can spot scams before they lose money. Most cybersecurity content is either too technical or too scary. I wanted to do the opposite, so I explain things in plain English and Hinglish, the way I would explain them to a friend or a family member.

By day I work as an IT Support Engineer, a role I have been in since July 2025. Fixing real problems for real users keeps me honest about what actually goes wrong online: weak passwords, rushed clicks, and fake messages that look just convincing enough. Alongside work, I am pursuing a BCA with a specialisation in Cloud Computing and Cyber Security, and I am also studying law through a BA LLB. The law side shapes a lot of my content, because knowing what the IT Act 2000, the DPDP Act 2023 and CERT-In directions say helps me explain your rights and what to do when something goes wrong.

Outside study and the day job, I build and share small projects on GitHub, like geoimg-osint, an image geolocation OSINT toolkit, and homelab-toolkit, a set of defensive Python utilities for home labs. I keep learning in public, and I would rather say "I am still learning this" than pretend to know everything. If you have a question, a correction or an idea for a topic, reach me on LinkedIn, GitHub or at madhavpandya@zoho.in.

CyberWithMadhav content is for awareness and education. Legal topics are explained in general terms and are not legal advice.

⚖ the law angle

Why law + tech

Most security content explains how attacks work. Studying law means I also look at what the law says about them, and what organisations must do to comply.

  • IT Act, 2000: unauthorised access (civil wrong under s.43; offence under s.66 if done dishonestly or fraudulently), identity theft (s.66C) and cheating by impersonation (s.66D)
  • Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023: consent, data fiduciary duties and breach obligations
  • CERT-In directions: incident reporting for organisations
  • GRC: turning these rules into policies, risk registers and audits
