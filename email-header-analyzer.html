Email Header Forensics

Got an email from "your bank", "the Income Tax Department" or "your HoD" that feels off? Paste its full headers to see whether it passed SPF, DKIM and DMARC, where it really came from, and whether the sender is pretending to be someone else.

Fully offline. Headers contain email addresses and IPs, so they are analysed only inside your browser. Nothing is uploaded or saved. The optional DNS lookup below sends just the sender's domain name to Cloudflare DNS, and only if you click it.

How to read the result

  • DMARC fail is the strongest spoofing signal: the domain in the From line did not authorise the message.
  • Reply-To on another domain, especially Gmail or another free service, means your reply goes to someone else. Common in fee, invoice and job scams.
  • Display name tricks: "State Bank of India" or "Income Tax Department" as the name, with an address that is not on an official domain such as sbi.co.in, a .bank.in address or incometax.gov.in.
  • The route: read Received lines from the bottom up. Only hops added by your own mail provider can be trusted; earlier ones can be forged.

Limits

  • The tool trusts the Authentication-Results line written by your mail provider. If you edit headers before pasting, the result changes.
  • A clean result means the headers look consistent. A real, hacked mailbox can still send a scam.
