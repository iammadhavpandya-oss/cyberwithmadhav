WhatsApp Account Takeover: How It Happens and How to Protect Yourself

When a scammer takes over a WhatsApp account, they do not just read chats. They message every contact asking for urgent money. Here is how it happens and how to lock your account in two minutes.

Quick answer

Most WhatsApp takeovers happen when someone tricks you into sharing the six digit registration code sent by SMS, or gets your account opened on a linked device. Turn on two step verification (a PIN that WhatsApp asks for when your number is registered on a new phone), add an email for recovery, and regularly check Linked devices. Never share the code, even with a friend.

How takeovers usually happen

The code trick. Someone tries to register your number on their phone. WhatsApp sends a six digit code to your SMS. Then a message arrives, often from a friend's already hacked account: "Sorry, I sent my code to your number by mistake, please forward it." The moment you share it, your account moves to their phone.

Fake support or prize messages. Messages pretending to be WhatsApp support or a contest ask for the code "to verify" you.

Linked devices. WhatsApp Web and desktop work by scanning a QR code with your phone. If someone gets your unlocked phone for a minute, or tricks you into scanning their QR code, they can read your chats from their computer.

Voicemail tricks. In some cases attackers request the code by voice call and try to access voicemail. Setting a voicemail PIN with your operator helps.

Protect your account in two minutes

  1. Open WhatsApp Settings, go to Account, then Two step verification, and turn it on. Choose a six digit PIN that is not your birthday.
  2. Add an email address when asked, so you can reset the PIN if you forget it.
  3. Go to Linked devices and log out of anything you do not recognise.
  4. Keep your phone locked with a strong screen lock.
  5. Turn on Security notifications so you know when something changes.

Menu names can change slightly with app updates, so if you cannot find an option, check the official WhatsApp Help Center.

Signs your account may be compromised

  • You are suddenly logged out with a message that your number is registered on another phone.
  • Friends ask why you sent strange messages or money requests.
  • You see unknown sessions in Linked devices.
  • Chats are marked as read before you open them.

If your WhatsApp is taken over

  1. Re register immediately. Open WhatsApp on your phone and verify your number with the SMS code. This logs the other person out. If they set a two step PIN, you may have to wait before you can register again, but they also lose access.
  2. Warn your contacts through other channels: calls, SMS or another app. Tell them not to send money to anyone using your name.
  3. Email WhatsApp support from the app or Help Center, explaining your account was stolen, with your number in international format.
  4. Report the incident on cybercrime.gov.in, especially if money was requested or lost. If anyone lost money, they should call 1930.
  5. Check your other accounts. If the attacker had your phone or email, change those passwords too.

When a friend asks for urgent money on WhatsApp

Hacked accounts are used to send messages like "Urgent, in hospital, please send 20,000 on this UPI, will return tomorrow." The message looks real because it comes from your friend's real account. Always call the person on their normal number, ideally a voice call where you can hear them speak naturally. With AI voice cloning becoming common, ask a question only the real person would know. Our post on AI voice cloning scams explains why.

What is changing in India

The Department of Telecommunications issued directions on 28 November 2025 requiring messaging apps that use Indian mobile numbers to keep accounts continuously linked to the active SIM in the device and to log out web sessions periodically. Reports in October 2026 said enforcement is planned from 1 January 2027. We explain what this means for you in our SIM binding explainer. Rules help, but your two step PIN is still your strongest personal defence.

One habit to teach everyone

"Do not give the code to anyone. Not ever." Not to a friend, not to support, not to a cousin. WhatsApp itself will never ask you for this code in a chat. Teach it to parents and kids in the same simple words.

Frequently asked questions

Can someone hack my WhatsApp just by knowing my number? Knowing your number alone is not enough in normal cases. They usually need the registration code, access to your unlocked phone or a linked device session. That is why the code and your screen lock matter so much.

Will WhatsApp support call me? Do not trust calls or messages claiming to be WhatsApp support asking for codes or PINs. Use the official Help Center inside the app.

Key takeaways

  • The WhatsApp registration code is like a house key. Never share it.
  • Two step verification adds a PIN that stops most takeovers.
  • Check linked devices regularly and log out of any you do not recognise.
  • If hijacked, re register your number, warn contacts and report it.
