Online Shopping and Refund Scams in India

Online shopping scams are among the most common scams Indians face. They use fake sites, social media ads and fake refund calls. A few habits keep you safe.

Quick answer

Buy from sites you already know, typed in yourself, not from a social media ad with a huge discount. Prefer cash on delivery or card on known sites. For a refund, use only the help section inside the official app. No genuine refund needs your UPI PIN, a QR scan or a screen sharing app. If you paid a scam, call 1930.

How common it is

The GSMA India Consumer Scam Report 2026 found online shopping scams were the most common type reported by Indian victims in its survey, at 33%. The same report says messaging apps and social media were the most common ways scams reached people.

Common shopping scams

  • Fake store: a smart looking site sells phones, shoes or sarees at very low prices. You pay, nothing arrives, or a cheap item arrives.
  • Social media ad shop: an Instagram or Facebook page with a "70% off, today only" deal and payment only by UPI.
  • Fake courier fee: a message says your parcel is held and asks for a small fee through a link.
  • Fake refund call: someone says your order failed and a refund is pending, then asks you to approve a request or install an app.
  • Fake customer care: numbers found in search results or comments that pretend to be the shopping site's helpline.

Checks before you pay

  1. Type the shop's address yourself. Do not trust the ad's link.
  2. Search the site's name plus words like "review" or "fraud", and read recent results.
  3. Be extra careful if the only payment option is UPI to a personal ID or QR code.
  4. Check the address bar spelling. Look alike names are common.
  5. If the deal is far better than every known store, treat that as the warning.

Is it that cheap? Then pause and check.

How real refunds work

On known shopping apps, refunds go back to your original payment method, and you track them inside the app's order page. You do not need to share an OTP, enter a UPI PIN, scan a QR code or install AnyDesk type apps. If you entered your PIN, money went out, it did not come in.

What to do if it already happened

  1. Call 1930 quickly and keep the acknowledgement number.
  2. Complete the complaint at cybercrime.gov.in with the transaction ID, website link and screenshots.
  3. Tell your bank or card issuer and ask about disputing the payment.
  4. Save the website address, ad screenshots, chat and payment proof before the page disappears.
  5. Report the ad or page to the social media platform.

Key takeaways

  • Huge discounts on unknown sites, especially from social media ads, are the main trap.
  • Refunds never need your PIN, a QR scan or remote access apps.
  • Get customer care numbers only from the official app or website.
  • Report fast on 1930 if money is lost.
