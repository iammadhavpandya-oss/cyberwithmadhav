How to Secure Your Parents' Phone: A 30 Minute Checklist

Parents are a big target for scammers, and they often trust messages and calls more than younger people do. One calm half hour with their phone can prevent a lot of trouble. Here is exactly what to do.

Quick answer

Set a screen lock, install updates, remove unknown apps (especially screen sharing and APK installs), turn on Play Protect on Android, set WhatsApp two step verification, lower UPI limits, save 1930 and the bank's official number as contacts, and teach three simple rules: no OTP, no PIN to receive money, and call you before acting on any scary message.

Why parents get targeted

Scammers know that many elders are polite on the phone, respect anyone who sounds official, and may not be familiar with how apps work. Common scams aimed at parents include fake bank KYC calls, electricity bill disconnection messages, digital arrest calls, fake pension or insurance calls and "your son is in trouble" calls. None of this means our parents are not smart. It means scammers are professionals, and our parents deserve a simple safety setup.

Step 1: Lock and update (5 minutes)

  • Set a screen lock: PIN, pattern or fingerprint. Avoid simple patterns like an L shape.
  • Go to settings and install system updates. Then open the app store and update all apps.
  • On Android, make sure Google Play Protect is on. It scans apps for harmful behaviour.
  • Turn off installing apps from unknown sources if it was enabled.

Step 2: Clean up apps (10 minutes)

Scroll through every app with them. Remove:

  • Apps they do not recognise or use.
  • Screen sharing and remote access apps unless you installed one deliberately for helping them.
  • Duplicate or suspicious "banking", "KYC" or "loan" apps.
  • Apps installed from APK files shared on WhatsApp.

Then check app permissions. A torch or wallpaper app should not need contacts, SMS or location.

Step 3: Protect WhatsApp and UPI (10 minutes)

  • WhatsApp: turn on two step verification with a PIN, add a recovery email (yours, if they do not use email), and check Linked devices. See our WhatsApp takeover guide.
  • UPI: set a lower daily limit if their apps support it, and turn on transaction notifications.
  • Banking alerts: make sure SMS alerts go to their phone, and if they agree, to yours as well through a joint arrangement with the bank.
  • SIM PIN: consider setting a SIM PIN so a stolen SIM cannot be used easily in another phone. Note it down safely.

Step 4: Save the right numbers (5 minutes)

Add these contacts:

  • Cyber Fraud 1930 with the number 1930
  • Bank Official with the number printed on the back of their card
  • You, at the top of favourites

Also install the Sanchar Saathi app or bookmark the portal. It lets you report fraud calls and messages, check mobile connections taken in their name and block a lost phone.

Three rules to teach (and repeat)

Keep it short and in their language:

  1. "OTP aur PIN kisi ko nahi batana." Nobody genuine will ask for it.
  2. "Paise lene ke liye PIN nahi lagta, scan nahi karna." Receiving money never needs a PIN.
  3. "Darr lage ya jaldi bole, toh pehle mujhe call karo." Any message or call creating fear or urgency means pause and call family.

Write them on paper and keep it near their charging point. Then do a quick role play: you pretend to be a "bank officer" and see how they respond. Laugh about it together, but make it stick.

A special word on digital arrest calls

Scammers pretending to be police, CBI or customs officials keep elders on video call for hours, claiming they are under "digital arrest". Tell your parents clearly: there is no such thing as digital arrest in Indian law, and real police do not demand money on video calls. Read and share our digital arrest scam guide.

Monthly check in

Once a month, spend five minutes: check for new apps, pending updates and unusual SMS. Ask, "Koi ajeeb call ya message aaya?" Keep it friendly, not like an exam. If something goes wrong, they should feel comfortable telling you immediately, not hide it out of embarrassment.

Frequently asked questions

Should I install a security app on my parents' phone? Built in protections like Google Play Protect, regular updates and app cleanup cover a lot. If you add a security app, choose a well known one from the official store, and avoid apps that ask for unnecessary permissions.

My parents get many spam calls. What helps? Report fraud calls on Sanchar Saathi's Chakshu, use the phone's built in spam call filtering, and register for Do Not Disturb preferences through their telecom operator. Most importantly, teach them that it is fine to cut a call that feels wrong.

What if they already shared details on a call? Act quickly: call the bank's official number to block cards or UPI, change passwords, and if money was lost, call 1930 at once. Reassure them that it is not their fault.

Key takeaways

  • Lock, update and clean up apps first.
  • Protect WhatsApp and UPI with PINs and sensible limits.
  • Save 1930 and official bank numbers as contacts.
  • Teach three simple rules and practise them with role play.
