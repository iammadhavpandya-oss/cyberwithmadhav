Fake mParivahan and e-Challan APK Scam

A message says you have a pending traffic challan and asks you to install an "mParivahan" app from a link. That file is often malware. Here is how to spot it and check your challan the safe way.

Quick answer

Never install an app from a link in a challan SMS or WhatsApp message. Check challans yourself on the official portal echallan.parivahan.gov.in, or in the NextGen mParivahan app installed from the Play Store or App Store, published by National Informatics Centre. If you already installed a file, turn on airplane mode, uninstall it and call your bank. If money is lost, call 1930.

How the scam works

  1. You get an SMS or WhatsApp message saying a traffic challan has been issued against your vehicle. Some messages include a vehicle number or a fine amount to look real.
  2. The message has a link that looks like a government site, or directly sends a file named something like "mParivahan.apk".
  3. Once installed, the fake app asks for many permissions, such as reading SMS, notifications and accessibility. Some versions hide their icon.
  4. It can read your OTPs and bank alerts, and may show a fake payment page to steal UPI or card details.

In November 2026, Hyderabad police registered a case after such SMS messages led people to an APK made to look like the real mParivahan app, as reported by the Times of India. Security researchers have also described malware spread through fake mParivahan and eChallan apps that captures UPI PINs, as covered by TechNadu.

Red flags in a challan message

  • It comes from an ordinary 10 digit mobile number.
  • It pushes you to download or install something.
  • It threatens extra fines or court action "today".
  • The link address is not on parivahan.gov.in. Look carefully, because fake addresses often add extra words or letters.
  • You do not even remember driving there.

How to check a challan the safe way

  • Type echallan.parivahan.gov.in yourself in the browser, and search with your vehicle number or challan number.
  • Or open the NextGen mParivahan app that you installed from the official Play Store or App Store. Check that the developer name is National Informatics Centre.
  • Never use a link or phone number from the message itself.

Do not install anything from a message. If you need to check a challan, open the official site yourself.

What to do if it already happened

  1. Turn on airplane mode straight away, so the app cannot send more data.
  2. Go to Settings, Apps, find the unknown app and uninstall it. If uninstall is blocked, check Settings for device admin apps and remove it there first.
  3. From a different, safe phone, call your bank on the number printed on your card and block UPI, net banking and cards if needed.
  4. Change your email and banking passwords from a safe device.
  5. If money left your account, call 1930 and file at cybercrime.gov.in. Our first hour guide has the full checklist.
  6. Report the SMS or WhatsApp number on Sanchar Saathi Chakshu, even if you did not lose money.

Tell your family in one line

"If a challan SMS arrives, do not open the link and do not install an app. Show it to me, or check on the official site yourself." For a wider habit on app safety, read how to check an app before you install it.

Key takeaways

  • A real challan never needs you to install an app from a message link.
  • Check challans only on echallan.parivahan.gov.in or the official NextGen mParivahan app.
  • A file ending in .apk sent on SMS or WhatsApp is a big red flag.
  • If you installed it, go offline, uninstall, secure your bank and report on 1930.
