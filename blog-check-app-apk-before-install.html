How to Check an App Before You Install It

Many scams now begin with one step: "install this app". Here is a two minute checklist that works for any app, so you and your family can say no to the dangerous ones.

Quick answer

Install apps only from the Google Play Store or Apple App Store, and check the developer name before you tap install. Never install an .apk file sent on WhatsApp, SMS or email. If a new app asks for accessibility, SMS or display over other apps permission without a clear reason, deny it and uninstall.

Why this matters

Fake challan, KYC, bank and wedding invite apps are common ways criminals get onto phones. Google's own help page says that if you download apps from unknown sources, "your device and personal information can be at risk" (Android Help).

The two minute checklist

  1. Where did it come from? If it came as a file or link on WhatsApp, SMS or email, stop. Search for the app yourself in the official store.
  2. Who made it? On the store page, check the developer name. Government apps usually show a government developer, such as National Informatics Centre for mParivahan. Banks publish apps under their own name.
  3. Does it look popular and real? Check the number of downloads and read recent reviews, especially low ratings.
  4. Is the name exactly right? Fake apps use look alike names and logos.
  5. What permissions does it want? A torch app does not need your SMS. A wallpaper app does not need accessibility.
  6. Why now? If a stranger or "customer care" is pushing you to install it while on a call, do not.

Permissions to think twice about

  • SMS: can read OTPs and bank alerts.
  • Accessibility: can read your screen and tap for you.
  • Display over other apps: can cover your real screen with a fake one.
  • Device admin: can make the app hard to uninstall.
  • Screen sharing or remote access: lets someone see and control your phone.

Phone settings to keep safe

  • Keep Google Play Protect on. Google says it is on by default and checks apps for harmful behaviour.
  • Keep "install unknown apps" off for WhatsApp, your browser and file manager.
  • Keep your phone updated.
  • Remove apps you no longer use.

For downloads on a computer, you can also learn to verify files with SHA-256 checksums.

What to do if you already installed something risky

  1. Turn on airplane mode.
  2. Uninstall the app. If it refuses, remove it from device admin first.
  3. From a safe device, change your email and banking passwords, and call your bank if any payment app is on the phone.
  4. If money is lost, call 1930 and file at cybercrime.gov.in.
  5. Read fake apps that steal your UPI PIN for the warning signs.

Key takeaways

  • Source first: official app stores only.
  • Check the developer, downloads and recent reviews.
  • APK files sent on chat are the biggest red flag.
  • Question every sensitive permission.

Official sources
