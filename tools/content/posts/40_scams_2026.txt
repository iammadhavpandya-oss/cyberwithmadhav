## who | Does this apply to me?
If your shop, clinic, coaching class or startup collects personal data in digital form, such as customer phone numbers, WhatsApp chats, delivery addresses, online forms or a customer database, the DPDP framework is relevant to you. The Act calls you a **Data Fiduciary** and your customer a **Data Principal**. For the bigger picture, read our [DPDP Rules timeline](blog-dpdp-rules-2025-timeline.html).

## timeline | The timeline in one line
The [Gazette notification of 13 November 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/267647.pdf) brought some provisions into force at once, some after one year, and most of the duties for businesses after eighteen months. That is why many guides, including ours, point to **13 May 2027**. Always check the latest official documents on the [MeitY DPDP page](https://www.meity.gov.in/documents/act-and-policies/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-gDOxUjMtQWa).

## checklist | The practical checklist
1. **Map your data.** Write down what personal data you collect (names, phone numbers, addresses, ID copies), why, where it is stored (phone, Excel, Google Sheet, billing software) and who can see it.
2. **Collect less.** Stop collecting what you do not need. If you do not need Aadhaar copies, do not keep them.
3. **Write a clear notice.** [PIB's summary](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2190014&lang=1&reg=3) says notices must be standalone, clear and simple, and explain the specific purpose. Use plain language your customers understand.
4. **Make withdrawal easy.** Customers should be able to withdraw consent as easily as they gave it, for example stop marketing messages.
5. **Show a contact.** PIB says data fiduciaries must display clear contact information for data questions. A named person and an email address is a good start.
6. **Lock it down.** Strong passwords and two factor login on email, cloud and billing tools. Give staff access only to what they need. Keep backups. See our [password guide](blog-strong-passwords-password-managers.html).
7. **Check your vendors.** If a software company or agency handles your customer data, ask how they protect it.
8. **Plan for a breach.** PIB says affected people must be informed promptly in plain language. Decide now who will act, how you will inform customers, and how you will contact the Data Protection Board.
9. **Delete on time.** Do not keep old customer data forever without a reason.
10. **Train your team.** One short talk on phishing and passwords prevents many leaks.

## rights | Requests from customers
The DPDP framework gives people rights to access, correct, update or erase their personal data. PIB says fiduciaries must respond to such requests within a maximum of **90 days**. Keep a simple register of requests and your replies.

## not-legal-advice | A note on this guide
This is a simplified, general guide based on official MeitY and PIB documents, not legal advice. Exemptions and details for specific businesses can differ, so read the official Rules or speak to a lawyer for your case. Our [intro to cyber law](blog-cyber-law-india-intro.html) explains how DPDP fits with the IT Act and BNS.

## if-breach | If a data leak already happened
1. Contain it: change passwords, remove exposed links, and close public sharing on files.
2. Write down what happened, when, and what data was involved.
3. Inform affected customers in plain language, and follow the official breach reporting process in the DPDP Rules.
4. If the leak involves a cyber crime such as hacking or fraud, report at [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in/). If customers lost money, they should call **[1930](tel:1930)**.

## quick-table | Which one, when?
- **1930 (National Cyber Crime Helpline):** call immediately when money has been lost to online fraud.
- **cybercrime.gov.in (National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal):** file the full complaint, for both financial fraud and other cyber crimes.
- **Chakshu on Sanchar Saathi:** report suspicious calls, SMS or WhatsApp messages, even if you lost nothing.
- **Your bank or payment app:** block cards or UPI and raise your own complaint about the transaction.

## money-lost | If you lost money
1. Call **[1930](tel:1930)**. You give your details and transaction details, and get an acknowledgement number by SMS.
2. Call your bank on the number printed on your card or in its official app. Block what is at risk and note the complaint number.
3. Complete the complaint at [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in/). The [Department of Financial Services](https://www.financialservices.gov.in/beta/en/page/csft) says complainants should do this within **24 hours** using the acknowledgement number.

Speed matters, because money moves quickly between accounts. Our [first hour guide](blog-first-hour-after-cyber-fraud.html) has a minute by minute plan.

## no-loss | If you only got a suspicious call or message
Use **Chakshu** on [Sanchar Saathi](https://sancharsaathi.gov.in/sfc/). It accepts fraud attempts by call, SMS or WhatsApp, such as fake KYC, electricity or gas disconnection, government impersonation and fake offers. The Sanchar Saathi site clearly says Chakshu is **not** for reporting money already lost. For that, use 1930 and cybercrime.gov.in. The site also asks you to report within 30 days for action.

## other-crimes | Hacked accounts, fake profiles and harassment
For crimes without money loss, such as a hacked social media account, a fake profile using your photos, or online harassment, file on [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in/). Also use the report tools inside the app, and secure your accounts with [two factor authentication](blog-two-factor-authentication-guide.html).

## bank | Why your bank still needs its own complaint
The police complaint and the bank complaint are separate. Your bank needs to know quickly so it can block your accounts and look at the transaction. Our [reporting guide](blog-report-cyber-fraud-india.html) explains the RBI's customer liability rules and why reporting quickly matters.

## safety | Stay safe while reporting
- Dial 1930 yourself and type cybercrime.gov.in and sancharsaathi.gov.in yourself.
- Do not use numbers from search ads or social media comments.
- Real police do not charge to recover money. Anyone asking for a fee is running a second scam.

## how | How the scam works
1. You get a WhatsApp video or voice call. The face or voice looks like your son, daughter, sibling or friend.
2. The call is short, often blurry, with "bad network" excuses.
3. They say there is an emergency: accident, police case, hospital, stuck abroad.
4. They ask you to send money now, to a UPI ID or account you do not know, and not to tell anyone.

Sometimes the call comes from an unknown number, and sometimes from the relative's real account after it was taken over. Our guide on [WhatsApp account takeover](blog-whatsapp-account-takeover.html) explains how that happens.

## why | Why it feels so real
Short clips of people's faces and voices are easy to find on social media. AI tools can turn them into a fake face or voice. Read more in our [AI voice cloning guide](blog-ai-voice-cloning-deepfake-scams-2026.html). Scammers add fear and hurry, so you do not stop to check.

## red-flags | Red flags
- Money is needed right now, and you must keep it secret.
- The video is very short, frozen, blurry or the lips do not match.
- They refuse to switch to a normal call or let you call back.
- Payment goes to a stranger's UPI ID or account.
- Someone else "takes over" the call, claiming to be police, doctor or lawyer.

## family-plan | A simple family plan
1. **Code word:** pick a word only your family knows. Ask for it on any emergency call.
2. **Call back rule:** always hang up and call the person back on the number already saved in your phone, or call another family member.
3. **No secrets rule:** agree that nobody in the family will ever say "don't tell anyone" about money.
4. **Lock your WhatsApp:** turn on two step verification, so it is harder to steal accounts.

Sabse pehle call kaato, phir khud call karo. Asli emergency mein 2 minute ka check kuch nahi bigaadta.

4. Report the calling number on [Sanchar Saathi Chakshu](https://sancharsaathi.gov.in/sfc/), and inside WhatsApp.
5. Tell the real relative so they can secure their account and warn others.

## different | How this is different from other QR scams
Our [UPI scams guide](blog-upi-scams-india.html) explains the "scan to receive money" trick and fake QR codes sent on chat. This post is only about **physical QR codes in public places**, where you really do intend to pay, but your money may go to the wrong person.

## where | Where swapped QR codes show up
- A sticker pasted over the shop's real QR code on the counter or stand.
- Parking lots, toll style counters and unattended kiosks with a printed "pay here" QR.
- Petrol pumps and busy stalls, where people pay quickly and do not read the screen.
- Temporary posters for events, donations or fees.

## check | The 10 second check for customers
1. Scan the QR.
2. **Read the payee name** shown in your UPI app before typing the amount or PIN.
3. Ask the cashier: "Ye naam sahi hai?" If it is the shop or the owner's name, go ahead.
4. Check the amount once more, then enter your PIN.
5. Show the payment success screen to the cashier, and check that they got the alert.

If there is no one to ask, such as an unattended parking QR, and the name looks unrelated, pay by another method.

## shop-owners | For shop owners and petrol pump staff
- Check your QR stand and stickers every morning. Look for anything pasted on top.
- Keep the QR behind the counter or under clear, fixed cover, where customers can scan but strangers cannot easily stick something on.
- Turn on the payment sound box or app alerts, and confirm every payment before handing over goods.
- If a customer says the name looked wrong, check your QR immediately.

## already-paid | What to do if it already happened
1. If you paid the wrong payee, tell the shop at once and note the time, amount and payee name.
2. Call **[1930](tel:1930)** and complete the complaint on [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in/) with the UTR number.
3. Raise a complaint with your bank and inside your UPI app.
4. If you are the shop owner, remove the fake sticker, photograph it as evidence, and report it as well.
