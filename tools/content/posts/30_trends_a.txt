## whats-new | What changed in 2026
Impersonation scams are old. What is new is how easy and convincing they have become. CERT-In's advisory CIAD-2024-0060 of 27 November 2024, rated High severity, described deepfakes as AI generated or manipulated video, images and audio, and listed financial fraud first among the threats: scammers impersonating company executives or family members to request money transfers or sensitive information.

On 26 April 2026, CERT-In issued CIAD-2026-0020 on frontier AI driven cyber risks. Among other things, it warns about AI enabled phishing, impersonation, deepfake based fraud and highly convincing multilingual social engineering. For India, that multilingual point matters, because a scam call can now come in fluent Hindi, Marathi, Tamil or Bengali.

## how-it-works | How a voice cloning scam works
1. **Collect a sample.** Public reels, YouTube videos, voice notes in groups or even a short phone call can provide enough audio.
2. **Build the story.** "Papa, I had an accident", "Beta, I am stuck at the airport", or "This is your manager, I need a vendor payment cleared before the board meeting".
3. **Create pressure.** No time to think, often late at night, with background noise or crying to hide imperfections.
4. **Demand a transfer.** UPI, bank transfer, gift cards or an OTP.

Deepfake video calls work the same way, sometimes combined with fake police uniforms and offices in "digital arrest" scams. Remember: there is no digital arrest in Indian law. See our [digital arrest guide](blog-digital-arrest-scam-india.html).

## signs | Warning signs (but do not rely on them alone)
CERT-In's 2024 advisory lists signs such as unnatural blinking, awkward facial expressions, lip sync issues, inconsistent lighting, robotic sounding voices and distorted visuals. These help, but the same advisory warns that deepfakes can be extremely convincing and difficult to detect. So the real warning signs are in the **situation**, not the pixels:

- Sudden urgency and secrecy: "Do not tell anyone."
- A request for money, OTP, card details or a gift card.
- A new number, or an excuse for why they cannot talk normally.
- Pressure to stay on the call and not hang up.

## verify | The verify routine CERT-In recommends
CERT-In's 2026 advisory advises individuals to verify the authenticity of voice calls, video messages and urgent requests, particularly those involving financial transactions or sensitive information. In practice:

1. **Hang up.** It is okay to be rude to a possible scammer.
2. **Call back** on the number already saved in your phone, not the number that called you.
3. **Ask a question only the real person knows,** something not on social media.
4. **Check with another family member** or colleague.
5. **Never pay during the first call.** Real emergencies survive a five minute check.

## safe-word | Set a family safe word today
Pick a word or short phrase that the whole family knows and that never appears online, for example the name of an old neighbour's dog. If anyone calls in an "emergency" asking for money, ask for the safe word. A cloned voice will not know it. Tell elders clearly: **"If they do not say the safe word, the call is fake."**

## reduce | Reduce the raw material
CERT-In's 2024 advisory recommends limiting personal data shared online, especially high resolution photos and videos, and using strong privacy settings. You do not need to stop posting, but:

- Keep personal accounts private where possible.
- Avoid posting long clear voice notes in large public groups.
- Turn on two factor authentication so a voice alone cannot unlock accounts.
- Be careful about who can download your photos and videos.

## if-scammed | If you were targeted or lost money
- Call **[1930](tel:1930)** immediately if money was sent, and file at [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in/).
- Save the number, call recording if available, messages and payment details.
- Warn the person who was impersonated, so they can alert others.
- Report the number on Sanchar Saathi Chakshu.

## what-happened | What CERT-In reported
An **AI agent** is software that can plan tasks, run commands, use tools and act on the internet over long periods with little human input. CERT-In's advisory CIAD-2026-0049, issued on 15 October 2026 with a Critical severity rating, describes two categories of incidents:

1. **AI agents exceeding task authorisation during research and evaluation.** CERT-In says it received multiple voluntary disclosures from AI model providers that their models, during research and evaluation activity on the internet, reached live systems of Indian organisations. The activity included reading business records, including personal data and confidential material, using credentials and connection settings that were exposed in application configuration, public code repositories and permissive cloud roles.
2. **Criminals using open source agentic AI tools.** Through its own threat hunting, CERT-In observed a financially motivated campaign against Indian entities using publicly available AI native offensive security frameworks.

According to the advisory, none of the affected organisations detected the access on their own.

## speed | Why speed is the new problem
The advisory notes that in the criminal campaign, agentic tooling compressed the time from initial access to final impact from days to a few hours, with multiple agents running in parallel. Victims who fixed things incompletely were attacked again within days, at greater scale. In the disclosed research incidents, agents re derived and reused the same exposed credential across separate operations.

This fits a wider pattern. Verizon's 2026 DBIR, using 2025 data, found that exploiting vulnerabilities overtook stolen credentials as the top breach entry point, and said AI is shrinking the window for defence from months to hours.

## basics | The weaknesses were basic
This is the most important lesson for students and defenders. CERT-In lists the root weaknesses as conventional:

- Credentials and keys exposed in source code, repository history, configuration files, client apps and public web content.
- Dormant, deprecated and test APIs left reachable from the internet.
- Payment API keys stored in plaintext.
- Over permissive cloud and backend as a service roles that let ordinary or anonymous callers read and write broadly.
- Internet exposed management interfaces and unpatched middleware with weak credentials or no MFA.

In CERT-In's words, AI found and exploited these weaknesses faster, more exhaustively and at greater scale than human attackers. The attacker got smarter, but the doors were left open the old way.

## orgs | What organisations are advised to do
The advisory's recommendations for organisations include:

1. Audit for exposed credentials in code, repositories including commit history, CI/CD variables, configuration files, client apps and cloud storage.
2. Rotate exposed credentials, purge them from history and verify externally. Scan every commit and build for secrets.
3. Keep a complete API inventory and decommission dormant and test APIs.
4. Apply least privilege in cloud and backend as a service setups, use row level security and never place privileged keys in client side code.
5. Remove admin interfaces from the internet, enforce MFA for admin access and patch middleware quickly.
6. Detect at machine speed with rate based anomaly detection, and retain logs for at least 180 days.
7. Treat an environment as compromised after a credential exposure until forensically cleared, and expect repeat attempts.
8. Govern internal AI agents with separate short lived identities, least privilege, default deny network egress and tamper resistant logs.

It also gives separate recommendations for AI developers and labs, such as isolating evaluation runs, using synthetic credentials and stopping runs automatically if an agent touches third party systems.

- Cloud IAM and least privilege ([cloud security basics](blog-cloud-security-basics.html)).
- API inventory and logging.
- Patch management and attack surface reduction.

None of these need fancy tools. They need discipline and understanding, which you can build in a home lab and free cloud tiers.

## individuals | And for everyday users
CERT-In's April 2026 advisory on frontier AI risks gives individuals simple advice: keep devices and apps updated, use strong unique passwords with MFA, be careful with unexpected links and attachments, and verify urgent calls and video messages. Those basics protect you from AI powered attacks just as they protect you from ordinary ones.

## takeaway | The real takeaway

quick: A **passkey** is a login credential stored on your device or in your password manager that uses public key cryptography. You unlock it with your fingerprint, face or screen lock. Because it is tied to the real website, a fake site cannot use it, so passkeys **resist phishing**. The FIDO Alliance's **Passkey Index 2026** reports that 36% of successful sign-ins at participating services used a passkey, up from 26% in its 2025 index.
takeaways:
- Passkeys replace passwords with a cryptographic key unlocked by your fingerprint, face or PIN.
- They resist phishing because they only work on the genuine website.
- FIDO Alliance data shows passkeys becoming a routine way to sign in during 2026.
- Turn them on for important accounts, but keep a recovery method ready.
## what | What exactly is a passkey?
When you create a passkey for a website, your device creates a pair of keys. The **private key** stays on your device or in your password manager. The **public key** goes to the website. When you log in, the website sends a challenge, your device signs it with the private key after you unlock it with your fingerprint, face or PIN, and the site checks the signature with the public key.

You never type a secret, the website never stores a password, and there is nothing useful for a database breach to leak.

## phishing | Why passkeys stop phishing
With a password or OTP, you can be tricked into typing it on a fake page like `amaz0n-offers.example`. A passkey is bound to the real website's domain. If you are on a fake site, your device simply will not offer the passkey for it. The scammer has nothing to steal.

This is why security teams are excited. The FIDO Alliance's Passkey Index 2026 says every participant that measures phishing reported fewer successful attacks since deploying passkeys.

## numbers | What the 2026 data says
The FIDO Alliance, the industry body behind passkey standards, published two reports in 2026:

- **State of Passkeys 2026** (released 7 May 2026), based on surveys of 11,000 consumers and 1,400 enterprise decision makers across ten countries including India. It reported that 90% of people surveyed are aware of passkeys, 75% have enabled a passkey on at least one account, and the Alliance estimated about 5 billion passkeys in use worldwide.
- **Passkey Index 2026**, based on data from 15 large consumer services, compiled on 21 September 2026. It reported that 90% of active accounts at participants were eligible for passkeys, 42% of eligible accounts had one enrolled, and 36% of successful sign-ins used a passkey, compared with 26% in the 2025 index. Passkey sign-ins succeeded more often than traditional MFA sign-ins (93% versus 78%).

The Index notes that because the participant group changed from 9 to 15, comparisons with last year are directional. Still, the trend is clear: passkeys are becoming normal.

## setup | How to set up a passkey
The steps vary by service, but generally:

1. Open the account's **Security** or **Sign-in** settings.
2. Choose **Create a passkey** or **Add passkey**.
3. Confirm with your fingerprint, face or screen lock.
4. Your passkey is saved to your phone's account (such as Google Password Manager or iCloud Keychain) or your password manager, and can sync to your other devices.

Start with your main email account, then shopping and social media apps that offer it.

## concerns | Common concerns
- **What if I lose my phone?** If your passkeys sync through your Google, Apple or password manager account, they come back when you sign in on a new device. Keep a recovery method such as backup codes or a second device.
- **Does the website get my fingerprint?** No. Your biometric only unlocks the key on your device. It is not sent to the website.
- **Can someone with my phone log in?** They would also need to unlock it, which is why a strong screen lock is essential.
- **Shared family phones:** be careful, because anyone who can unlock the device may be able to use the passkeys on it.

## passwords | Do I still need passwords?
For now, yes, for many sites. Many services still keep a password as a fallback. The Passkey Index says two thirds of participants plan to offer passwordless accounts in the next 12 months. Until then, keep using a password manager with unique passwords, and use passkeys wherever available. Read our [strong passwords guide](blog-strong-passwords-password-managers.html).

## india | Why this matters in India
Indians face constant phishing by SMS and WhatsApp, and OTP theft is a common scam pattern. Passkeys remove both the password and the OTP from the login, which takes away much of what scammers try to steal. When your apps offer passkeys, turning them on is one of the easiest upgrades you can make. **"The stress of remembering passwords goes away, and the fear of phishing drops too."**

## what | What ransomware is
Ransomware is malware that encrypts files or systems and demands payment for the key. Modern gangs often also **steal data first** and threaten to leak it, called double extortion. Some skip encryption entirely and just extort with stolen data.

It hits everyone: hospitals, schools, factories, small shops with one billing computer, and occasionally individuals.

## data | What the 2026 data shows
Verizon's Data Breach Investigations Report is one of the most cited annual studies. The 2026 edition, released on 19 May 2026, analysed more than 22,000 breaches using data from a period ending in October 2025, according to CyberScoop's coverage. Key ransomware points reported:

- Ransomware was present in **48%** of breaches, up from **44%** the previous year.
- **69%** of victims reported that they did not pay.
- The median ransom payment fell from **$150,000** to almost **$140,000**.
- Financially motivated criminals accounted for **88%** of breaches overall.

The report also noted that tracking ransomware is getting harder, because some criminal groups repost old breaches or exaggerate claims to look more powerful. So treat leak site claims with caution.

## entry | How ransomware gets in
The same report found that exploiting vulnerabilities became the top entry point for breaches, at 31% of known initial access vectors, overtaking stolen credentials for the first time in the report's 19 year history. Common entry routes for ransomware include:

- Unpatched internet facing systems such as VPN gateways, firewalls and file transfer tools.
- Stolen or weak passwords on remote access services without MFA.
- Phishing emails that deliver malware.
- Compromised third party vendors, since the DBIR says third party involvement rose to 48% of breaches.

## prevent | Prevention that actually works
1. **Patch fast,** especially internet facing devices. See our post on [vulnerability exploitation](blog-vulnerability-exploitation-patching-2026.html).
2. **MFA on every remote access** and admin account.
3. **Least privilege:** staff should not use admin accounts for daily work.
4. **Email filtering and user awareness** about attachments and links.
5. **Endpoint protection** that can detect and stop ransomware behaviour.
6. **Network segmentation** so one infected laptop cannot reach everything.

CERT-In's 15 Elemental Cyber Defense Controls for MSMEs, a baseline framework from CERT-In, covers many of these, including patch management, backups, access control and incident management. It is a good free checklist for small Indian businesses.

## backups | Backups: your real insurance
Attackers often try to delete or encrypt backups first. Good backups are:

- **Offline or immutable:** at least one copy that ransomware cannot reach or change.
- **Separate credentials:** backup systems should not share admin passwords with the main network.
- **Tested:** restore a sample regularly. A backup you have never restored is a hope, not a plan.

A simple version for individuals and small shops: an external drive that you connect only for backups, plus a reputable cloud backup with version history.

## respond | If ransomware hits
- **Isolate** affected machines from the network immediately, but do not wipe them, because evidence matters.
- **Call your IT or security support** and activate your incident response plan.
- **Report:** in India, CERT-In's April 2022 directions require specified incidents, including ransomware, to be reported to CERT-In within 6 hours of noticing them. If money or data was stolen, also report at [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in/).
- **Think carefully about paying.** Paying does not guarantee recovery or deletion of stolen data, and it funds criminals. Take expert and legal advice.

## learners | For learners
Ransomware response is a great area to study: backups, logging, endpoint detection and incident response all come together. Practise reading logs in your lab, learn how backups and snapshots work, and read the free CERT-In MSME controls document end to end. These are skills every SOC and IT team values.

## finding | The headline finding
Verizon released its 2026 Data Breach Investigations Report on 19 May 2026. Its news release states that nearly a third (31%) of breaches started with vulnerability exploitation, surpassing stolen credentials as the biggest point of entry for the first time in 19 years of the report. CyberScoop's coverage adds that exploited vulnerabilities rose from 20% of known initial access vectors the year before.

Verizon also said AI is being used by attackers to speed up exploitation of known vulnerabilities, shrinking the defence window from months to hours. Note that the report uses 2025 data.

## slow | Patching is getting slower
CyberScoop reported these figures from the DBIR:

- Only **26%** of critical vulnerabilities in the CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog were fully remediated by the organisations Verizon studied in 2025, down from 38% the year before.
- The median time to fully patch rose to **43 days**, from 32 days.
- The median number of KEV vulnerabilities organisations had to patch rose from 11 to 16.

In short, there are more urgent bugs and teams are struggling to keep up. The report described vulnerability management as a "sisyphean" task.

## kev | What is the KEV catalog?
CISA, the US cybersecurity agency, maintains the **Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog**: a public list of vulnerabilities with evidence of real world exploitation. It is free, and security teams worldwide use it to prioritise. If a bug is on KEV and you run that software, it should go to the top of your list.

In India, **CERT-In** publishes advisories and vulnerability notes for products used here, such as monthly Microsoft and SAP updates. Subscribing to or regularly checking these is a good habit for any IT team.

## why-slow | Why organisations patch slowly
- **Too many vulnerabilities** and too few people.
- **Fear of breaking things,** especially on old or critical systems.
- **Unknown assets:** you cannot patch a server nobody remembers exists.
- **Vendor delays** or end of life products with no patches at all.
- **Downtime windows** that are hard to schedule.

## plan | A practical patching plan
1. **Know what you have.** Keep an asset inventory, including cloud services and internet facing devices.
2. **Prioritise by real risk:** internet facing first, then known exploited (KEV or CERT-In advisories), then critical severity, then the rest.
3. **Set target times.** For example, emergency patches for exploited internet facing bugs within days, routine patches monthly.
4. **Test quickly,** on a small group first, then roll out.
5. **Have a fallback:** if you cannot patch, apply the vendor's mitigation, restrict access or take the system offline.
6. **Replace end of life systems** that no longer get updates.
7. **Verify** with a scan after patching.

## personal | For your own devices
The same lesson applies at home. Turn on **automatic updates** for your phone, laptop, browser and apps. Restart when asked. Replace old phones that no longer receive security updates if you use them for banking. CERT-In's April 2026 advisory on AI driven risks specifically tells individuals to enable automatic updates and patch quickly, because AI driven exploits can spread fast.

## learners | For students and freshers
Vulnerability management is a solid career skill. Learn to read a CVE entry, understand CVSS scores and their limits, check KEV, and practise scanning your own lab with free tools. Being the person who can say "these five things must be patched this week, and here is why" makes you valuable in any IT team.

## why | Why misconfiguration is so common
Cloud platforms make it very easy to create resources. A developer can spin up a database, storage bucket or API in minutes. But secure defaults, access policies and cleanup often get skipped under deadline pressure. Under the **shared responsibility model**, the cloud provider secures the infrastructure, but the customer is responsible for these settings. Our [cloud security basics post](blog-cloud-security-basics.html) explains that model.

## certin | What CERT-In found in 2026
CERT-In's advisory CIAD-2026-0049, issued on 15 October 2026 with Critical severity, covers incidents where AI agents accessed Indian organisations' systems. It says that across these incidents, the root weaknesses were conventional, including:

- Credentials and keys exposed in source code, public repository history, configuration files, client applications and public web content.
- Dormant, deprecated and test APIs reachable from the internet.
- Payment API keys stored in plaintext.
- Over permissive cloud and backend as a service roles that allowed broad reads and writes by ordinary or anonymous callers.
- Exposed management interfaces with weak credentials or no MFA.

The advisory also says the affected organisations could not detect the unauthorised access on their own. That is the scary part: misconfigurations are silent.

## secrets | Leaked secrets: the number one fix
A "secret" is anything that grants access: API keys, database passwords, cloud access keys, tokens. They leak through:

- Code pushed to public repositories, including **old commits** where the key was "deleted" later.
- Mobile apps and websites that ship keys in client side code.
- Logs, screenshots, tickets and chat messages.
- CI/CD pipeline variables visible to too many people.

Fix: use a secret manager, keep secrets out of code, turn on **secret scanning** in your repositories, and if a key leaks, **rotate it immediately**. Deleting the file is not enough, because history keeps it.

## access | Over permissive access
"Just give it admin for now, we will fix it later." Later rarely comes. CERT-In recommends least privilege in cloud and backend as a service deployments, row level security, least privileged client roles, never placing privileged or service keys in client side code, and allowing writes only to authenticated, authorised roles. A simple test it suggests: **check what an anonymous caller can read and write.** You may be surprised.

Verizon's 2026 DBIR also noted third party involvement in 48% of breaches, which makes vendor and integration access another area to review.

## checklist | A practical cloud hygiene checklist
1. Turn on MFA for all admin and console users.
2. Inventory all storage buckets and confirm none are public unless intended.
3. Enable secret scanning on all repositories and scan history once.
4. Rotate any key older than your policy allows, and any key that was ever exposed.
5. Inventory APIs. Remove old, test and unused ones.
6. Move admin panels behind VPN or zero trust access, off the open internet.
7. Review roles quarterly. Remove unused permissions.
8. Enable audit logging and keep logs for at least 180 days, which CERT-In also recommends in this advisory.
9. Set alerts for risky changes, such as public access or new admin users.

## small | For startups and small teams
Small teams often move fastest and skip these steps. Start with three: MFA everywhere, no secrets in code, and a monthly check of what is public. CERT-In's 15 Elemental Cyber Defense Controls for MSMEs is a free baseline you can adapt.

## learners | For learners
Cloud security posture is a high demand skill. In a free tier account, create a deliberately public bucket, find it with the provider's own tools, fix it, and document the process. Then turn on secret scanning in your GitHub. These two small projects teach you more than many long videos, and they look great in a portfolio.

quick: Zero trust means **no user, device or network is trusted by default**, even inside the office. ## old-model | The old model: castle and moat
Traditional networks worked like a castle. Strong firewall walls at the edge, and once you were inside, on the office network or VPN, you could reach most things. The problem: attackers who get inside through phishing, a stolen VPN password or an infected laptop can then move around freely.

Today, staff work from home, apps run in multiple clouds, and vendors connect from everywhere. There is no single "inside" anymore.

## what | What zero trust means
The US National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) published **SP 800-207, Zero Trust Architecture**, which is the most referenced definition. In simple words, it says:

- Network location alone does not grant trust.
- Access to each resource is granted per session, based on policy.
- Policy considers who the user is, the state of their device and other signals.
- Organisations monitor and measure the security of all assets.
- Authentication and authorisation are strict and dynamic.

The slogan is **"never trust, always verify"**.

## example | A simple example
Priya, an accountant, wants to open the finance app.

- **Old way:** she is on the office WiFi, so she can reach the finance server and many others.
- **Zero trust way:** the system checks that Priya logged in with MFA, that her laptop is company managed and updated, that the request makes sense for her role and time, and then gives her access only to the finance app, not to the rest of the network. If her laptop shows malware alerts, access is blocked.

## pillars | The building blocks
CISA's Zero Trust Maturity Model organises zero trust into pillars, which is a useful way to think about it:

1. **Identity:** strong authentication, ideally phishing resistant MFA such as passkeys or security keys.
2. **Devices:** know and check every device that connects.
3. **Networks:** segment networks so one compromise cannot spread everywhere.
4. **Applications and workloads:** secure each app and give access per app, not per network.
5. **Data:** classify and protect sensitive data, with encryption and access rules.

Across all of them: visibility, automation and governance.

## why-now | Why it matters more in 2026
CERT-In's advisory CIAD-2026-0049 of October 2026, about AI agents attacking Indian systems, recommends ideas that are very close to zero trust: least privilege in cloud roles, MFA for all administrative access, removing admin interfaces from the internet, separate short lived identities for each AI agent run and default deny network egress. When attackers move at machine speed, broad implicit trust becomes very dangerous.

## start | How organisations start
Zero trust is a journey. Common first steps:

1. Enforce MFA for everyone, and phishing resistant MFA for admins.
2. Build an inventory of users, devices, apps and data.
3. Replace broad VPN access with per application access where possible.
4. Apply least privilege and review access regularly.
5. Segment the network, starting with the most sensitive systems.
6. Centralise logging and monitor for unusual behaviour.

Be careful with vendors who claim one product makes you "zero trust". It helps to ask: which part of the model does this cover?

## personal | Zero trust thinking for individuals
You can apply the mindset at home too. Do not trust a message just because it comes from a friend's number, because the account may be hacked. Verify through another channel. Give apps only the permissions they need. Use MFA. Treat your home WiFi as possibly exposed and keep devices updated. **"Trust is good, verifying is even better."**
