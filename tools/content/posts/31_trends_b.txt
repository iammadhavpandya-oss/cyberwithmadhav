## basics | DPDP in one paragraph
The **Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023** governs how organisations process digital personal data in India. Individuals are called **Data Principals**. Organisations deciding why and how data is processed are **Data Fiduciaries**. The Act gives individuals rights, such as access, correction and erasure, and puts duties on organisations, such as lawful processing, purpose limitation, security safeguards and breach notification. The **Data Protection Board of India** handles violations and can impose penalties. MeitY told the Lok Sabha in August 2026 that penalties can go up to ₹250 crore, depending on factors like the nature of the breach and harm caused.

## timeline | The three phase timeline
The commencement notification G.S.R. 843(E), dated 13 November 2025, and MeitY's statements describe three phases:

- **Phase 1, from 13 November 2025:** definitions, provisions about the Data Protection Board and related sections came into force on publication. This is about setting up and operating the Board.
- **Phase 2, one year later, from 13 November 2026:** provisions about **Consent Managers** came into force. On 14 November 2026, PIB reported that MeitY launched the portal dpbi.gov.in for eligible companies to apply for registration.
- **Phase 3, eighteen months after notification, from 13 May 2027:** the core duties of Data Fiduciaries, the rights of Data Principals, and the Board's inquiry and penalty processes come into force.

## consent-managers | What is a Consent Manager?
A Consent Manager is an entity registered with the Data Protection Board that lets people give, manage, review and withdraw consent through one accessible, transparent and interoperable platform. According to PIB's 14 November 2026 release, only companies incorporated in India with adequate technical, operational and financial capacity, including a net worth of at least ₹2 crore, can apply. The Board will publish the list of registered Consent Managers on its website.

For everyday users, this could eventually mean one dashboard to see and withdraw consents given to different apps, instead of hunting through each app's settings.

## may-2027 | What starts on 13 May 2027
From this date, most of the protections people care about apply. Key duties for organisations include:

- **Clear notice:** PIB's release on the Rules says Data Fiduciaries must give standalone, clear and simple consent notices explaining the specific purpose of data collection.
- **Valid consent** for processing where consent is the basis, and easy withdrawal.
- **Reasonable security safeguards,** such as encryption or masking, as MeitY described to Parliament.
- **Breach intimation:** Rule 7 of the DPDP Rules requires informing affected individuals without delay, and informing the Board without delay, with a detailed report within 72 hours unless the Board allows more time.
- **Erasure** of personal data when consent is withdrawn or the purpose is served, unless the law requires retention.

## individuals | What it means for you as an individual
Until 13 May 2027, most of these rights are not yet enforceable under the DPDP framework, but you can already practise good habits: share less data, read consent screens, and remove apps you do not use. After May 2027, you should expect clearer notices, the ability to withdraw consent more easily, and breach notifications if your data is affected.

## business | What businesses should do now
Six months is not a lot of time. A practical checklist:

1. **Map your data:** what personal data you collect, why, where it is stored and who can access it.
2. **Fix notices and consent flows** to be clear, specific and easy to withdraw.
3. **Strengthen security:** access control, encryption, logging and secret management. CERT-In's 2026 advisories show how often basic weaknesses lead to data exposure.
4. **Prepare a breach response plan** with a 72 hour reporting track for the Board.
5. **Review vendors** who process data for you.
6. **Train staff.** Most data incidents involve people and processes, not just technology.

For a more detailed, plain language checklist aimed at small shops and startups, see our [DPDP checklist for small shops and startups](blog-dpdp-checklist-small-business.html).

## students | For law and security students
DPDP sits exactly where law meets security, and it creates real jobs in privacy, GRC and security engineering. If you are studying law like me, or cybersecurity, learn both sides: read the Act and Rules on official sites, and understand how security controls make compliance possible. Our [intro to cyber law](blog-cyber-law-india-intro.html) is a good starting point. This post is general information, not legal advice.

quick: On **28 November 2025**, the Department of Telecommunications issued directions under the Telecom Cyber Security Rules, 2024 (as amended) to app based communication services that use Indian mobile numbers, including WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal and others. Apps must stay **continuously linked to the active SIM** in the device, and **web sessions must log out at least every 6 hours**. News reports in October 2026 said enforcement is set for **1 January 2027**.
takeaways:
- Messaging apps must stay linked to the active SIM of the registered number.
- Web and desktop sessions must log out at least every 6 hours and re link by QR code.
- The aim is to stop scams run from abroad using Indian numbers.
- Reports say enforcement starts 1 January 2027. Keep your SIM active and in your phone.
## what | What the directions say
According to PIB's release of 1 December 2025, DoT observed that some app based communication services let users keep using the service even when the SIM linked to the number is not in the device. It said this was being misused for cyber fraud, especially from outside India.

DoT issued directions on 28 November 2025 under the Telecom Cyber Security (TCS) Rules, 2024 (as amended) to services including **WhatsApp, Telegram, Snapchat, Arattai, Sharechat, Josh, Jiochat and Signal**. The directions require them to:

1. Ensure the app is **continuously linked to the SIM card** associated with the registered mobile number, making it impossible to use the app without that specific, active SIM in the device.
2. Ensure **web instances log out periodically, not later than every 6 hours**, with an option to re link using a QR code.

The original directions gave 90 days for implementation and 120 days for a compliance report.

## why | Why the government says it is needed
PIB's release explains that accounts on messaging apps continue to work even after the SIM is removed, deactivated or moved abroad. That allows anonymous scams, remote digital arrest frauds and government impersonation calls using Indian numbers. Long lived web sessions also let fraudsters control accounts from far away after authenticating once.

The release notes that device binding and automatic logout are already standard in Indian banking and UPI apps, and cites cyber fraud losses exceeding ₹22,800 crore in 2024. It also clarifies that the direction does not affect users who are roaming with their SIM present in the handset.

## timeline | The timeline in 2026 and 2027
The New Indian Express reported on 22 October 2026 that DoT is set to make SIM binding mandatory from **1 January 2027**, with a compliance deadline of 31 December 2026, quoting a DoT official. The same report said web and desktop versions will face mandatory logouts every six hours, and mentioned reports that Snapchat is exempt. Treat details from news reports as subject to change, and check official DoT and PIB updates as the date approaches.

## users | What may change for you
- **Keep your SIM in the phone where you use the app.** If you use WhatsApp on a phone without the SIM for that number, for example a second phone on WiFi, it may stop working.
- **Keep the number active.** If your SIM is deactivated for not recharging, the app linked to it may be blocked.
- **WhatsApp Web and desktop users** may need to scan the QR code again more often.
- **Travellers:** the release says roaming with the SIM in your handset is not affected.
- **Changing phones:** move the SIM with the app, and follow the app's official instructions.

## scams | Will this stop scams?
It should make some scams harder, especially those that use Indian numbers from abroad or hijacked web sessions. But scammers adapt. They may move to other channels, use mule SIMs or social engineering. So your personal defences still matter:

- WhatsApp two step verification PIN (see our [takeover guide](blog-whatsapp-account-takeover.html)).
- Check linked devices regularly.
- Verify money requests by calling the person.
- Report suspicious calls and messages on Sanchar Saathi's Chakshu.

## debate | The debate
Some users and experts have raised concerns about convenience, multi device use and privacy, while telecom operators have supported the move, according to news coverage. As a learner, it is a good case study in the trade off between security, usability and privacy, a balance that appears in almost every security decision.

## check | Quick checklist before January 2027
1. Is the SIM for your WhatsApp number active and inside the phone you use?
2. Do you have two step verification turned on?
3. Do you know which devices are linked to your account?
4. Have you told elders that "app band ho gaya" messages asking them to click links or share codes are scams?

Rules help, habits protect. Dono zaroori hain.

## what | What RBI decided
In its Statement on Developmental and Regulatory Policies of 7 February 2025, RBI announced exclusive internet domains, **bank.in** for banks and **fin.in** for the wider financial sector, to fight fraud in digital payments. Its circular RBI/2025-26/28 of 22 April 2025 operationalised the .bank.in domain through the **Institute for Development and Research in Banking Technology (IDRBT)**, authorised by NIXI under MeitY as the exclusive registrar.

All commercial banks, primary urban cooperative banks, state cooperative banks and district central cooperative banks were advised to complete migration **no later than 31 October 2025**.

## progress | How far has it gone?
Business Today reported on 31 October 2025 that several major banks, including SBI, HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Axis Bank, Kotak Mahindra Bank and Punjab National Bank, had moved to .bank.in addresses, with old addresses redirecting. The Economic Times later reported NIXI's CEO saying more than 400 banks had moved, that some missed the deadline, and that all major entities had complied.

## why | Why a special domain helps
Phishing sites copy bank logos and designs perfectly. What they cannot easily copy is a **restricted domain**. Anyone can register something like `sbi-kyc-update.com` or `hdfc-netbanking-login.in` in minutes. But .bank.in can only be registered by eligible banks through IDRBT. So the domain becomes a simple trust signal that does not depend on how real the page looks.

## check | How to check a bank link correctly
Read the domain **from right to left**, starting from the end of the address before the first single slash:

- `https://www.examplebank.bank.in/login` ends in `.bank.in`. Good sign.
- `https://examplebank.bank.in.secure-login.com/` actually ends in `secure-login.com`. Fake.
- `https://bank.in-examplebank.com/` ends in `in-examplebank.com`. Fake.
- `https://examplebank-bank.in/` ends in `-bank.in`, which is not the same as `.bank.in`. Suspicious.

The safest habit is still to **type your bank's address yourself or use the official app**, rather than clicking links in SMS, email or WhatsApp. You can also check a suspicious link with the [link checker on this site](index.html#link-tool) without opening it.

## limits | What .bank.in cannot protect you from
- **Scam calls** pretending to be bank staff. A phone call has no domain.
- **Fake apps** and APK files sent on WhatsApp.
- **Remote access tricks,** where a caller asks you to install screen sharing apps.
- **Lookalike tricks** if you do not actually read the domain.
- **Fake customer care numbers** found through search. Use the Sanchar Saathi trusted contact details service or your bank's official app.

## family | Teach your family in one line
**"Bank ki website ka address .bank.in pe khatam hona chahiye. Warna mat kholo."** Then show them the address bar of your bank's real site on their phone, so they know what it looks like. For other common SMS tricks, read our [phishing SMS guide](blog-spot-phishing-sms-india.html).

## learners | For learners
This is a neat real world example of DNS used as a security control, which connects to what you learned in [networking basics](blog-networking-basics-for-cybersecurity.html). Try looking up your bank's domain with `nslookup` and viewing its TLS certificate in the browser. Understanding how domains, certificates and phishing interact is useful in SOC and awareness roles alike.
