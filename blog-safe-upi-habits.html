Safe UPI Habits: 10 Simple Rules to Protect Your Money

UPI made payments easy for everyone, including scammers. The good news is that a handful of habits protects you from almost every common UPI fraud. Share these with your family.

Quick answer

Enter your UPI PIN only to send money, never to receive it. Read the payee name before you pay, never install screen sharing apps on a caller's request, keep your phone locked and updated, and turn on transaction alerts. If money is lost, call 1930 and your bank immediately.

Rules 1 to 3: the PIN rules

1. PIN means money is going out. Your UPI PIN is like your signature on a cheque. You type it only when you are paying. Anyone who says "enter your PIN to receive the refund" is lying.

2. Never share your PIN, OTP or card details. Not with family friends on the phone, not with "bank officers", not with "customer care". Real bank staff will never ask for them.

3. Do not scan QR codes to receive money. A QR code is for paying. If a buyer on an online marketplace sends you a QR code "to receive the advance", scanning it will send money from your account.

Rules 4 to 6: check before you pay

4. Read the payee name. On the final confirmation screen, check that the name matches the shop or person. Fake stickers pasted over a shop's QR code are a known trick.

5. Decline payment requests you did not start. NPCI asked banks and UPI apps to stop person to person collect requests from 1 October 2025, but merchant requests still exist. If a payment request pops up and you did not start it, decline it.

6. Be suspicious of "sent by mistake". If someone calls saying they sent money to you by mistake, first check your bank app, not the SMS, because fake credit SMS are common. If money really came, ask them to contact their bank, which handles wrong transfers properly.

Rules 7 and 8: protect the phone

7. No screen sharing or remote access apps on request. Apps that let someone see or control your screen are useful for genuine IT support, but scammers use them to watch you type your PIN. If a caller asks you to install one, hang up.

8. Lock and update. Use a screen lock, set an app lock on UPI apps if available, and keep your phone's operating system and apps updated. Install apps only from the official Play Store or App Store.

Rules 9 and 10: limits and alerts

9. Turn on alerts and check them. Keep SMS and app notifications for every debit. If you see a payment you did not make, act immediately.

10. Set sensible limits. Many banking and UPI apps let you set a daily transaction limit. For elderly parents who make small payments, a lower limit reduces the damage if something goes wrong. Use a separate account with a small balance for daily UPI if that suits you.

Finding real customer support

A very common trap is searching online for "XYZ bank customer care number" and calling the first result. Scammers post fake numbers in search results, social media comments and fake websites. Always take support contacts from:

  • the official app (help or support section)
  • the back of your debit or credit card
  • the official website, typed by you

The Sanchar Saathi portal also offers a trusted contact details service for verifying bank customer care numbers.

If money is lost: speed and your rights

Act in this order: call 1930, inform your bank and block the affected card or UPI, then complete the complaint on cybercrime.gov.in with every transaction ID.

The RBI circular of 6 July 2017 on customer liability says that when an unauthorised electronic transaction happens because of a third party breach (not your negligence), and you report it to your bank within three working days, your liability is zero. Reporting later can increase your liability, so speed matters. If the loss happened because you shared your PIN or OTP, the rules are different, which is one more reason never to share them.

Make it a family habit

Print or write these rules on one page and stick it near where your parents keep their phone. Practise with them: "If someone calls and says enter PIN to get refund, what will you do?" The right answer is "Call cut karke beta ko phone karungi." For more examples, read our detailed guide to UPI scams.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to keep a large balance in my UPI linked account? Many people prefer linking UPI to an account with a smaller balance for daily spending, and keeping savings in a separate account. It limits damage if something goes wrong.

Someone sent me money and now asks me to return it. What should I do? First confirm in your bank app that money really arrived. If it did, ask the sender to raise it with their bank. Do not send money to a new number or UPI ID they give you.

Key takeaways

  • UPI PIN is only for paying. Receiving money never needs a PIN or a QR scan.
  • Always read the payee name on the confirmation screen.
  • Never share screen, OTP or PIN with anyone, including "bank staff".
  • Report fraud fast: 1930, cybercrime.gov.in and your bank.
