DPDP Checklist for Small Shops and Startups

You do not need a big legal team to start getting ready for India's data protection law. This is a practical, plain language checklist for small businesses. It is general information, not legal advice.

Quick answer

Start now with five steps: list the personal data you collect, cut what you do not need, write a clear notice for customers, put basic security in place, and plan what to do in a breach. Most duties under the DPDP Act start on 13 May 2027, eighteen months after the Rules were notified on 13 November 2025.

Does this apply to me?

If your shop, clinic, coaching class or startup collects personal data in digital form, such as customer phone numbers, WhatsApp chats, delivery addresses, online forms or a customer database, the DPDP framework is relevant to you. The Act calls you a Data Fiduciary and your customer a Data Principal. For the bigger picture, read our DPDP Rules timeline.

The timeline in one line

The Gazette notification of 13 November 2025 brought some provisions into force at once, some after one year, and most of the duties for businesses after eighteen months. That is why many guides, including ours, point to 13 May 2027. Always check the latest official documents on the MeitY DPDP page.

The practical checklist

  1. Map your data. Write down what personal data you collect (names, phone numbers, addresses, ID copies), why, where it is stored (phone, Excel, Google Sheet, billing software) and who can see it.
  2. Collect less. Stop collecting what you do not need. If you do not need Aadhaar copies, do not keep them.
  3. Write a clear notice. PIB's summary says notices must be standalone, clear and simple, and explain the specific purpose. Use plain language your customers understand.
  4. Make withdrawal easy. Customers should be able to withdraw consent as easily as they gave it, for example stop marketing messages.
  5. Show a contact. PIB says data fiduciaries must display clear contact information for data questions. A named person and an email address is a good start.
  6. Lock it down. Strong passwords and two factor login on email, cloud and billing tools. Give staff access only to what they need. Keep backups. See our password guide.
  7. Check your vendors. If a software company or agency handles your customer data, ask how they protect it.
  8. Plan for a breach. PIB says affected people must be informed promptly in plain language. Decide now who will act, how you will inform customers, and how you will contact the Data Protection Board.
  9. Delete on time. Do not keep old customer data forever without a reason.
  10. Train your team. One short talk on phishing and passwords prevents many leaks.

Requests from customers

The DPDP framework gives people rights to access, correct, update or erase their personal data. PIB says fiduciaries must respond to such requests within a maximum of 90 days. Keep a simple register of requests and your replies.

A note on this guide

This is a simplified, general guide based on official MeitY and PIB documents, not legal advice. Exemptions and details for specific businesses can differ, so read the official Rules or speak to a lawyer for your case. Our intro to cyber law explains how DPDP fits with the IT Act and BNS.

If a data leak already happened

  1. Contain it: change passwords, remove exposed links, and close public sharing on files.
  2. Write down what happened, when, and what data was involved.
  3. Inform affected customers in plain language, and follow the official breach reporting process in the DPDP Rules.
  4. If the leak involves a cyber crime such as hacking or fraud, report at cybercrime.gov.in. If customers lost money, they should call 1930.

Key takeaways

  • Most business duties start 18 months after the 13 November 2025 notification.
  • Know what personal data you hold, why, and where.
  • Clear notices, easy consent withdrawal and contact details matter.
  • Basic security and a breach plan protect both you and your customers.
