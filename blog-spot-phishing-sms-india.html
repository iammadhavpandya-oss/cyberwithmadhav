How to Spot a Phishing SMS in India: KYC, Electricity Bill and Parcel Scams

Most scams in India still start with one short message. If your family can spot a fake SMS in five seconds, they block a huge share of fraud before it starts.

By Madhav Pandya · Published 15 November 2026 · Last reviewed 15 November 2026 · 4 min read

SMSphishingKYCfamily

Quick answer

A phishing SMS creates urgency ("your power will be cut tonight", "account blocked today") and pushes you to click a link, call a number or share an OTP. Real banks, electricity boards and couriers do not ask for your PIN, OTP or card details by SMS. Do not click. Open the official app or website yourself, and report the message on Sanchar Saathi Chakshu.

Why SMS scams work so well

SMS feels official. Bank alerts, OTPs and delivery updates all come by SMS, so a fake one blends in. Scammers also know that many people read messages quickly on a small screen, while busy, and act before thinking.

The goal of almost every phishing SMS is one of three things: get you to open a fake website that steals your login or card, get you to call a fake helpline where someone talks you into paying, or get you to install an app that gives them control of your phone.

Common fake SMS you will see

  • KYC update: "Dear customer, your bank account will be blocked today. Update KYC at this link." Banks do not block accounts through an SMS link.
  • Electricity bill: "Your power connection will be disconnected tonight at 9:30 pm as previous bill was not updated. Call this officer." The number goes to a scammer, who may ask you to install a screen sharing app.
  • Parcel held: "Your parcel is on hold due to incomplete address. Pay a small fee." The small fee page steals card details.
  • Reward points expiring: "Your credit card reward points worth a large amount expire today. Redeem now."
  • Traffic challan: fake e-challan links that look like government sites.
  • Job or part time income: "Earn from home by liking videos." This usually ends in a task scam.

Five second red flag check

Teach your family these five questions:

  1. Is it scaring me or tempting me? Fear (block, cut, penalty, arrest) or greed (prize, refund, cashback) is a warning sign.
  2. Is there a link or a phone number to act on? Genuine organisations want you to use their official app or website.
  3. Is it asking for OTP, PIN, CVV or a password? That is always a scam.
  4. Does the link look odd? Spelling mistakes, extra words like "-kyc-update", or short links.
  5. Was I expecting this? If you did not order a parcel or apply for anything, be extra careful.

If even one answer worries you, do nothing from the message itself.

Can I trust the sender name?

Legitimate business SMS in India come from registered sender headers, often shown as letters with a short suffix. This helps, but do not treat the header alone as proof. Scammers also send messages from ordinary 10 digit mobile numbers, and they keep inventing new tricks. The safest habit is the same every time: verify independently. Open your bank app, the official electricity board website, or the courier's official app, which you find yourself, not from the SMS.

What to do with a suspicious SMS

  • Do not click the link, do not call the number, do not reply.
  • Report it on Chakshu in the Sanchar Saathi portal or app run by the Department of Telecommunications. Your report helps block numbers that target others too.
  • Delete the message after reporting.
  • If you want to check a link without opening it, use a link checker such as the free one on this site, and still do not enter any details.
  • Tell family members about the pattern. A two line WhatsApp to your family group can save someone.

If you already clicked or shared something

Stay calm and act quickly:

  1. If you entered card, UPI or bank details, call your bank on the number printed on your card or in the official app and block the card or UPI.
  2. If you installed an app on a caller's instruction, turn on airplane mode, uninstall the app, and change your banking and email passwords from another device.
  3. If money left your account, call 1930 immediately and then file a complaint at cybercrime.gov.in. Our first hour guide has the full checklist.

Practise with your family

Spotting scams is a skill, and skills need practice. Sit with your parents for 10 minutes and play Scam ya Nahi?, our free spot the scam game in Hinglish. Show them two or three real scam messages from your own phone. Make a family rule: "Koi bhi link ho, pehle mujhe forward karo." It sounds simple, but it works.

Key takeaways

  • Urgency plus a link or phone number is the classic phishing SMS pattern.
  • Never share OTP, PIN, CVV or passwords, whoever asks.
  • Check things yourself in the official app or website, not through the message.
  • Report suspicious SMS on Sanchar Saathi Chakshu, and call 1930 if money is lost.

Official sources

  • Sanchar Saathi (DoT): Chakshu, report suspected fraud messages
  • cybercrime.gov.in: National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal
  • 1930: National Cyber Crime Helpline
