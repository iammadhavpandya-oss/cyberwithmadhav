India's Cyber Fraud Numbers, 2021 to 2025: What the Official Data Shows

Cyber fraud is often discussed with scary guesses. Here we stick to official numbers shared by the Ministry of Home Affairs in Parliament, explain what they mean, and what they do not.

Quick answer

According to MHA data from the I4C's reporting systems, financial fraud complaints on the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal rose from 2,62,846 in 2021 to 24,02,579 in 2025, and the amount reported rose from ₹551 crore to ₹22,495 crore (slightly below ₹22,848 crore in 2024). By 30 June 2026, the CFCFRMS behind the 1930 helpline had saved more than ₹11,158 crore in more than 32.80 lakh complaints. Fast reporting matters.

The official numbers, year by year

The Minister of State for Home Affairs shared this data in a written reply in the Rajya Sabha, published by PIB. It covers financial fraud complaints on the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (NCRP) and amounts reported through NCRP and the Citizen Financial Cyber Fraud Reporting and Management System (CFCFRMS), both run by the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C):

  • 2021: 2,62,846 complaints, ₹551 crore reported
  • 2022: 6,94,446 complaints, ₹2,290 crore reported
  • 2023: 13,10,357 complaints, ₹7,465 crore reported
  • 2024: 19,18,835 complaints, ₹22,848 crore reported
  • 2025: 24,02,579 complaints, ₹22,495 crore reported

The reply notes that the data is dynamic in nature, so later reports may show slightly different figures.

Money saved through 1930

CFCFRMS was launched in 2021 for immediate reporting of financial frauds, and the toll free 1930 helpline helps people register complaints quickly. According to the same PIB reply, by 31 December 2025, more than ₹8,189 crore had been saved in more than 23.61 lakh complaints.

A later MHA reply in the Lok Sabha (Unstarred Question 251) updated this: by 30 June 2026, more than ₹11,158 crore had been saved in more than 32.80 lakh complaints. The same reply said that from 2021 to 2025, there were more than 65.89 lakh financial fraud complaints on NCRP, more than ₹55,050 crore reported, more than ₹8,189 crore marked as lien, and more than 1,95,760 FIRs registered.

How to read these numbers correctly

  • More complaints does not only mean more crime. Awareness of 1930 and the portal has grown, so more people report. Both factors likely play a role, and the data alone cannot separate them.
  • Amount reported is not final loss. It is what victims reported, and some money is frozen or recovered later.
  • "Saved" means amounts put on hold or prevented from being siphoned, as described by MHA. Returning money to victims is a separate legal process handled by banks and courts.
  • Police action is a state subject. MHA notes that converting complaints into FIRs, arrests and chargesheets is handled by state and union territory police.

The trend in plain words

Complaints grew roughly nine times in five years. The amount reported jumped sharply until 2024 and stayed at a similar level in 2025. Meanwhile, the amount saved through quick reporting is growing. The message is simple: fraud is huge, and speed of reporting makes a real difference.

Why fraud keeps growing

The official data does not list causes, but government releases point to recurring patterns. The DoT's December 2025 release on SIM binding mentioned phishing, investment, digital arrest and loan scams, and cross border fraud using Indian numbers. CERT-In's 2026 advisories warn about AI powered phishing and deepfake impersonation. Our posts on AI voice cloning and SIM binding cover these.

What this means for you

  1. Save 1930 in every family member's phone today.
  2. Learn the first hour routine, in our first hour checklist.
  3. Report every fraud, even small amounts. Reports help police link cases and block mule accounts.
  4. Report suspicious calls and SMS on Sanchar Saathi's Chakshu, even if you did not lose money.

For students and researchers

If you write about cyber fraud, use primary sources like PIB releases and Parliament answers, quote the exact period and wording, and avoid mixing numbers from different systems such as NCRB crime data and NCRP complaint data. Being accurate with numbers is a big part of being trusted in this field.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I find the latest official numbers? Look for MHA replies in Parliament (published on sansad.in and PIB) and official I4C releases. Be careful with social media posts that do not cite a source.

Do these numbers include all cyber crime? No. These figures are financial fraud complaints on NCRP and amounts reported through NCRP and CFCFRMS. Other cyber crimes and NCRB crime statistics are separate datasets.

Key takeaways

  • Financial cyber fraud complaints grew about nine times between 2021 and 2025.
  • The amount reported in 2025 was ₹22,495 crore, close to the 2024 figure.
  • The 1930 system had saved more than ₹11,158 crore by 30 June 2026.
  • The data is dynamic, and reported amounts are not the same as final losses.
